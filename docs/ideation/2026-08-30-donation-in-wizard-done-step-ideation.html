Donation in the wizard Done step

A donation ask can work here because value has just become tangible. The winning version should feel like an invitation to keep Windpeek alive—not a toll booth placed after success.

Date · 2026-08-30 Topic · donation-in-wizard-done-step Focus · effective, smart and enjoyable donation prompt Mode · repo-grounded
Evidence

Grounding Context

Codebase Context. The complete state currently contains only a success heading, one confirmation sentence and one primary Done button (web/src/components/installer/InstallerComplete.vue:6–13). The flow reaches it only after configuration and forecast verification (InstallerPanel.vue:43–51, 210–225), so this is a genuine “value received” moment rather than an arbitrary marketing interruption.

The panel is compact: copy is capped at 13.5rem, actions sit at the bottom, and the existing visual language already includes secondary buttons and a thin progress bar (installer.css:98–145, 191–216). Any funding story therefore needs to be concise and native to this small surface.

1

A two-stage “Keep Windpeek flying” goal

Goal & transparencyConfidence 88%Complexity Medium

Description. Show one small annual funding bar with a meaningful first marker: “Running costs covered”. Money after that marker funds a named stretch outcome such as “next device support” or “better forecasts”. The marker makes break-even understandable; the bar gives surplus a positive purpose.

Running costs covered
Basis
reasoned: A raw donated-total answers “how popular is this?” but not “why should I help?”. A cost marker plus a stretch outcome answers both necessity and impact, while remaining honest if funding exceeds costs.
Rationale
This is the smartest use of your progress-bar instinct. It turns money into a small story: survive first, improve second.
Downsides
Needs current, verifiable cost numbers and a clear time window. Stale totals damage trust more than having no meter.

Recommended shape: “€340 of €600 this year · €240 keeps hosting and forecast services running.” Add a marker at €240 and label the remaining segment with one concrete stretch goal.

2

Celebrate first, ask second

Ask timing & hierarchyConfidence 94%Complexity Low

Description. Keep “Done” as the primary, focused action. Place a compact support card above it with a secondary “Support Windpeek” link or button. The success confirmation remains the emotional headline; donation never becomes the price of leaving the wizard.

Basis
direct: The current state autofocuses a single primary Done button (InstallerComplete.vue:11–12), and the stylesheet already has a visually subordinate secondary-button treatment (installer.css:127–145).
Rationale
Preserving an obvious exit protects the generosity of the product. The ask benefits from the completion high without taking the completion hostage.
Downsides
A secondary action will convert less than a dominant CTA. That is a healthy trade if goodwill matters more than short-term clicks.
3

Public feature unlocks

Goal & transparencyConfidence 86%Complexity Medium

Description. Put one or two feature markers after the operating-cost marker: when collective donations reach a marker, you commit to building and publicly releasing that feature for every Windpeek owner. Good candidates are bounded and visible, such as multi-spot button navigation or support for an additional display—not open-ended promises like “a better app”.

Basis
direct: The repo already contains a bounded multi-spot/button-navigation plan and the installer currently targets one reTerminal E1002. These are tangible feature shapes whose work and user value can be explained without inventing a vague roadmap.
Rationale
This makes donating feel collective: supporters are not buying private access, they are helping everyone get the next capability. It is more motivating than a generic total because progress has a visible consequence.
Downsides
A reached marker becomes a real public commitment. Estimate conservatively, show “funds the work” rather than a release date, and never list more features than you can realistically deliver.

Important framing: “Unlocked for everyone when funded.” Do not say “released when funded” if the feature is already finished; holding completed work back would feel artificial. A clean sequence is: running costs → feature A → feature B.

4

Let people choose what Windpeek was worth

Donation action & choiceConfidence 78%Complexity Low

Description. Open the donation page with three friendly presets—roughly a coffee, a lunch, and a “back the project” amount—plus custom. In the wizard, keep only one CTA: “Support Windpeek”. Avoid forcing the amount decision into the already compact panel.

Basis
reasoned: The user has just completed a meaningful hardware installation, so a value-framed contribution is easier to answer than an abstract “donate”. Moving amount choice to the payment surface avoids crowding the 13.5rem copy area.
Rationale
Presets reduce decision friction while preserving agency. The wording can remain human: “Worth a coffee?” rather than “Choose a tier”.
Downsides
Requires a good external payment page and sensible regional currency behavior.
5

Show supporters, not a vanity total

Goal & transparencyConfidence 75%Complexity Medium

Description. Pair the goal with a modest human signal: “Supported by 37 Windpeek owners this year.” Keep the euro total tied to the cost goal; use people-count only to show community participation.

Basis
reasoned: A lifetime euro total can look boastful or suspiciously small. A bounded supporter count provides social reassurance without implying that popularity itself is the reason to donate.
Rationale
It makes a solo/open maker project feel alive, especially when the count is still small enough to feel personal.
Downsides
Needs a clear definition of “supporter” and consent-safe aggregation. Very low counts can suppress rather than encourage.
6

A playful wind-powered thank-you

Delight & personalityConfidence 68%Complexity Medium

Description. Make the funding bar visually Windpeek-native: a tiny gust fills the line toward the operating-cost marker, or the existing completion flag gains a subtle extra flourish beside the support invitation. The data stays literal; only the presentation is playful.

Basis
direct: The complete phase already uses an animated state icon, while the installer uses a 3px pill-shaped progress language (installer.css:200–216). A restrained funding visualization can reuse those conventions.
Rationale
The moment becomes memorable and feels designed for Windpeek rather than pasted in from a fundraising template.
Downsides
Easy to over-polish. Motion must not delay exit, obscure the actual numbers or run for reduced-motion users.
